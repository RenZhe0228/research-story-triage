# Evidence Claim Boundaries

Use four evidence levels for every important claim.

## Four-Level Classification

| Level | Use when | Example wording |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Supported by current evidence | The data directly test the claim with suitable design and analysis. | "The treatment increased measured SOC stock in the sampled depth interval." |
| Reasonable inference | The claim is consistent with data but not directly proven. | "These patterns suggest a possible link between microbial traits and SOC chemistry." |
| Needs verification | The claim is plausible but requires additional data or validation. | "Activity data are needed to test whether encoded pathways are expressed." |
| Cannot be concluded | The design or data cannot support the claim. | "These MAGs drive carbon sequestration." |

## General Boundary Rules

- Observational association does not prove causality.
- Genomic potential does not equal gene expression, enzyme activity, metabolic flux, or ecosystem process rate.
- Model prediction does not imply mechanism.
- Feature importance does not prove biological control.
- SEM, PLS-PM, and path modeling with small n are hypothesis framing, not causal proof.
- Differential abundance does not prove functional importance.
- Taxonomic shifts do not automatically imply functional shifts.
- Functional gene abundance does not automatically imply process rate.
- Machine-learning claims require independent validation or clear cross-validation without leakage.

## Carbon And Ecosystem Rules

- Carbon sequestration claims require carbon stock, persistence, depth, bulk density, temporal baseline, and uncertainty.
- Carbon sink enhancement claims require additionality, permanence, leakage risk, and a defined measurement boundary.
- Field-scale claims require spatial representativeness and uncertainty propagation.
- Incubation results support potential process behavior under assay conditions, not direct field-scale rates.

## Causal Language Guardrails

Avoid "drive", "control", "determine", "regulate", "mediate", and "govern" unless the design supports causality. Prefer bounded wording:

- "is associated with"
- "is consistent with"
- "may contribute to"
- "is a candidate predictor of"
- "supports the hypothesis that"
- "suggests, but does not prove"

## Downgrade Instructions

If a claim is unsupported, downgrade the story rather than only softening the wording when the unsupported claim is central to the novelty, title, abstract, or figure sequence.
