# Red Flags

Use this checklist to identify weak stories, fatal flaws, and reviewer objections.

## Story And Question

- The paper only lists analyses without a testable question.
- The main conclusion depends on one weak correlation.
- The strongest result is actually a limitation.
- The story repeats a prior paper using the same dataset.
- The manuscript has multiple unrelated mini-stories and no central claim.
- The title claims more than the strongest figure proves.

## Design And Statistics

- The sample size is too small for the proposed model.
- The same metadata were copied across replicates and treated as independent.
- Site-level variables are analyzed as sample-level independent measurements.
- SEM, PLS-PM, or path modeling is overparameterized.
- Machine learning is used only for decoration.
- Cross-validation leaks site, time, subject, or treatment information.
- Model performance is reported without uncertainty or validation.

## Evidence And Mechanism

- The manuscript claims mechanism without perturbation, time series, isotope tracing, transcriptomics, enzyme assays, or process measurements.
- Feature importance is treated as biological control.
- Differential abundance is treated as functional importance.
- Taxonomic shift is treated as functional shift.
- Gene abundance is treated as expression, enzyme activity, flux, or process rate.

## Soil Carbon And Microbiome

- The title claims carbon sequestration but the data only show SOC concentration.
- The microbiome result is not connected to a measurable ecosystem endpoint.
- MAGs, CAZymes, KEGG, or METABOLIC annotations are used to claim active carbon metabolism.
- SOC stability is claimed from a proxy without persistence or turnover evidence.
- Carbon sink enhancement is claimed without SOC stock, depth, bulk density, temporal baseline, permanence, and uncertainty.
- Regional carbon sequestration is claimed without spatial representativeness and uncertainty propagation.
